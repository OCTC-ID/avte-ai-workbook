Part 1 · Before we meet · Chapter 2

Try it on something just for you

About 15 minutes

You’ll leave with

A feel for working back and forth with an AI tool, from the learner’s seat, and four short reflections to share anonymously.

This one isn’t about work, on purpose. When you use AI on something you already know well, your expertise quietly does the checking for you. Using it on something personal puts you where your students are: trying to get help with something that matters to you, without being the expert.

Pick something real

Choose something you actually want help with this week. A few ideas:

  • Plan a weekend trip or a holiday visit
  • Put together meals for a busy week
  • Write a message you’ve been putting off
  • Understand something you’ve always wondered about
  • Sort out a home project, a garden, or a hobby

Have a real conversation

Don’t stop at the first answer. Ask a follow-up, push back on something, or ask it to change direction at least twice. As you go, notice the moments when you trusted it, when you didn’t, and when you hesitated before typing something in.

Try it

Step 1

Get a starting prompt

Not sure how to start? Fill this in and copy the prompt into your AI tool. Change anything you like.

Who, when, budget, what you’ve tried. Skip anything you’d rather not share.
Your prompt

Step 2

Work with it for about 10 minutes

Follow up at least twice. Ask it to change something, explain something, or try again.

Step 3

Share four quick reflections

Post your answers to these four questions on the class Padlet wall. The Padlet link is in the Week 7 module in Canvas. Posts are anonymous, so be as honest as you like. You can jot notes here first; they stay private in this browser.

You’ll know you’re ready when

You’ve had a real back-and-forth with the tool and posted all four answers on the Padlet wall.
