Talk with your students about AI

About 15 minutes

Works toward · Create

Design an AI-informed teaching approach and a clear course AI-use expectation for your context.

This chapter puts your course AI-use expectation into practice through conversations with your students.

You’ll leave with

A plan for the first conversation about AI in your course, and a calm first step for when a student’s work doesn’t seem like their own.

Start early, and start with questions

The best time to talk about AI is the first week, before anything goes wrong. Ask your students how they already use it. You’ll learn a lot, and it tells them the topic is open for discussion.

Then walk them through your course expectation and the reason behind it. Connect it to the job. They’ll work with software that touches client and patient records, and they’ll be the one checking that a number is right before it reaches a patient.

If you used AI to build something in the course, say so. It shows them the same openness you’re asking for.

When something seems off

At some point a student’s work won’t sound like them. Here’s a calm way to handle it.

  • Don’t rely on an AI detector. These tools aren’t reliable enough to base a decision on. They flag real student writing, and research has found they flag writing by non-native English speakers more often.
  • Compare it with what you know. Look at their earlier work, their lab performance, and what they say in class.
  • Have a conversation. Ask the student to walk you through how they did the work and why they made the choices they did. Keep it about their learning. Most of the time, this conversation tells you what you need to know.
  • Follow your school’s process. If you still have concerns, use your program’s academic integrity steps. Find out what they are before you need them.
  • Look at the assignment, too. If several students lean on AI for the same task, the task may be easy to hand off. Chapter 5 can help.

Try it

Step 1

Plan your first conversation

The first week of class is best. Name the day or the activity.
For example: “What’s one thing AI has helped you with?” and “When has it given you a wrong answer?”
Connect it to the job they’re training for.

Step 2

Be ready for when something seems off

Start with curiosity. For example: “Walk me through how you worked on this.”
If you don’t know yet, write down who you’ll ask.
You’ll know you’re ready when

You know when you’ll have the first conversation and what you’ll ask, and you know your first step and your school’s process if a student’s work seems off.
