Your students are making the same choices

In Chapter 2, some of you paused before you pasted something in. That pause was a judgment call about what to share and what to trust. Your students make those same calls every week, usually without anyone telling them what you expect. A clear expectation helps them make good ones.

It also gives them practice for the job. As vet techs they’ll decide what goes into software that touches client and patient records. Your course is a good place to start that habit.

Start with what’s already decided

Check whether your program or school already has an AI policy. If it does, your course expectation works inside it. If it doesn’t, you’re writing the first one for your course.

Three simple labels

Labels give you and your students the same words for where AI fits.

  • No AI. Students do this work without AI because the thinking is the point.
  • AI-supported. Students can use AI along the way, for example to brainstorm or to quiz themselves. The work they turn in is their own.
  • AI-integrated. Using AI is part of the task, and students show how they used it.

Most courses use more than one. Your course expectation sets the starting point, and each assignment says where it’s different.

Write it for your students

Use plain words and say why. When students can see the reason for an expectation, they’re more likely to follow it, and more likely to ask when they’re unsure.

If you use AI to help build your course, say that too. It shows students the same openness you’re asking of them.
